## Objective
The primary objective of this page is to properly analyze the various strengths and weaknesses of two engineering portfolio's in order to determine what constitutes a "proper" engineering portfolio. In order to incorporate such elements for personal use. 

## Analyze:

## PART A: 

### Portfolio A: 
a. **Navigability** 

Portfolio A was found to be easily navigable. Multiple hyperlinks were included within the page, minimizing the need for the viewer to scroll to view any of the assorted projects. The location of these hyperlinks added to viewing ease, as their positioning at the top of the page consequently make them among the first items viewed on the main page. 

b. **Reproducibility:**

Portfolio A tended to present product timelines that would be reproducible with few challenges. In terms of a colleague being able to complete the same project without *any* questions, is doubtful. Though there is strong reasoning displayed throughout many of the various projects, some steps of the design process, especially those describing error, used vague language that did not properly elaborate on how the aforementioned errors affected that portion of the product development. 

c. **Evidence of Reasoning**

Portfolio A exhibited strong evidence of reasoning throughout all documentation. Each project includes sequential documentation that explains how the engineers initial approach of the problem interacted with potential issues that arose, subsequent solutions, and final thoughts as the deliverable was finished. 

d. **Professional Tone:**

Portfolio A had struggles with professional tone. Professional in this sense defined as a mostly impersonal assessment of the project accomplished as a whole.  While the text was easily digestible, there's various instances where analysis fell into the realm of something you'd see in a personal notepad as opposed to a document that someone would turn into their superior as a report.

### Portfolio B: 
a. **Navigability** 

Portfolio B has a easily navigable structure. The two pages that comprised the entire portfolio were separated by an slider embedded into the page, reducing the inconvenience of having to manage two individual tabs upon viewing. All relevant projects are conveniently accessible while including information relevant to employers such as images, project objectives, results, and in some cases a in depth report detailing the abbreviated information 

b. **Reproducibility:**

Portfolio B tended to have in depth descriptions of projects, but tended to lack enough information for a colleague to reproduce. This may be because many projects listed on the portfolio are under various copyrights and/or patents. 

c. **Evidence of Reasoning**

Portfolio B presented little to no evidence of reasoning throughout. As only the final result and objective of a listed project were included 

d. **Professional Tone:**

Portfolio B exhibited a technical, concise tone throughout. It did accomplish the earlier defined definition of professional.
